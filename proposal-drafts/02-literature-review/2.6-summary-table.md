## 2.6 Summary of Reviewed Works

Table 2.3 summarises the 33 reviewed studies in the order of the themes in Section 2.4. Numerical findings appear only where they were checked against the abstract or text of the study.

**Table 2.3: Summary of selected reviewed works**

| S/N | Author(s) and year | Aim/problem | Method/technique | Dataset/tools | Key findings | Limitations/gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ding et al. (2024) | Cut large-model calls while keeping quality | Router predicts the quality gap between a small and a large model; threshold | MixInstruct | Up to 40% fewer large-model calls in the tested setting | Two models; no failure or load |
| 2 | Ong et al. (2025) | Cut cost by routing between a strong and a weak model | Router trained on human preference data | Chatbot Arena; MT-Bench, MMLU, GSM8K | 3.66× cost-saving ratio versus GPT-4 at 95% of its MT-Bench quality (Table 6) | Two-model setting; distribution shift |
| 3 | S. Chen et al. (2024) | Match each query to a suitable model | Query and model representations with dual contrastive learning | Multi-task model outcomes | +2.76% in distribution; +1.90% out of distribution (abstract) | Small model pool; training complexity |
| 4 | Shirkavand et al. (2025) | Cost-aware routing over changing model pools | Shared prompt–model embedding; cost-aware contrastive training; nearest-neighbour retrieval | EmbedLLM, MixInstruct, RouterBench | Reported to cope with unseen models and prompts | Static cost; no live load or failover |
| 5 | Shnitzer et al. (2023) | Select models using benchmark results | Router learned from benchmark correctness labels | Public benchmark outcomes | Benchmark labels can train routers | Benchmark-specific labels; preprint |
| 6 | Šakota et al. (2024) | Cheapest model that solves each query | Meta-model predicts per-model success; assignment under a budget | HELM tasks; OpenAI models | About 63% cost reduction (abstract) | Price-only cost; no latency or load |
| 7 | Mohammadshahi et al. (2024) | Cheapest adequate model per prompt from a large pool | Correctness predictor plus cost-aware selector | MMLU | Reported accuracy gains at equal cost (figures not reused) | One benchmark; preprint |
| 8 | Lu et al. (2024) | Select one model using reward supervision | Reward-guided router distillation | Instruction queries; reward-model scores | Rewards can supervise pre-generation choice | Depends on reward quality |
| 9 | Varangot-Reille et al. (2026) | Generalise routing from past performance | Few-shot router with performance retrieval | Routing benchmarks | Simple nearest-neighbour is competitive; 1% history in one test | Label and retrieval setting |
| 10 | Xiong et al. (2024) | Measure LLM confidence elicitation | Prompting, sampling and aggregation strategies | QA and reasoning datasets | Verbalised confidence tends to be overconfident | Needs a generation per query |
| 11 | Da & Kalyvianaki (2026) | Choose model and instance jointly under load | Weighted quality–latency–cost score with load tracking | Qwen2.5 models; multi-GPU cluster | Joint choice reported faster than a quality-only router at high load | One model family; offline labels; preprint |
| 12 | Chen et al. (2025) | Meet per-stage service targets across replicas | Token-budget scheduler; admission control; routing | vLLM; ShareGPT, HumanEval, ToolBench | Higher capacity than compared systems | Needs profiling; few replicas; preprint |
| 13 | Toniolo et al. (2026) | Route across regions using cache, load and network latency | Latency cost model with online weight tuning | SGLang; three regions | Better tail latency than session affinity on held-out windows | Single model; synthetic trace; preprint |
| 14 | Chen, L. et al. (2024) | Reduce API spending | Learned quality-based cascade | Scored API task outputs | Up to 98% cost reduction (arXiv abstract) | Needs labelled similar data; sequential calls |
| 15 | Dekoninck et al. (2025) | Unify routing and cascading | Optimisation view; cascade routing with estimators | RouterBench, SWE-Bench and others | Gains of up to 8% (RouterBench) and 14% (SWE-Bench) | Estimator quality decisive; static costs |
| 16 | Ding et al. (2025) | Route without over-using the largest model | Predict reward for (model, samples) choices | MT-Bench; eight LLMs | Reported large cost reduction for a small quality drop (figure not reused) | One judge; static prices |
| 17 | Aggarwal et al. (2024) | Decide when to escalate a weak answer | Self-verification and POMDP-based escalation | QA benchmarks | Self-verification informs escalation | Answer-dependent; sequential inference |
| 18 | Gupta et al. (2024) | When should a small model defer | Learned deferral on token-level uncertainty | FLAN-T5 models; NLP tasks | Learned rules more consistent than simple confidence scores | Needs token probabilities |
| 19 | Jiang et al. (2023) | Combine models’ answers | Pairwise ranking and generative fusion | Candidate response sets | Ranking and fusion combine strengths | Many generations; out of scope here |
| 20 | Agrawal et al. (2024) | Better latency evaluation for LLM serving | Token-deadline fluidity index; black-box API probing | vLLM, hosted APIs | Tail token timing can differ from averages | Preprint |
| 21 | Goel et al. (2026) | Co-schedule mixed latency classes | Deadline-aware scheduling per QoS class | Llama3-8B, Qwen-7B; Azure traces | More capacity than siloed serving | Small models; own SLO classes |
| 22 | Zhong et al. (2024) | Maximise goodput under latency targets | Prefill–decode disaggregation; placement search | OPT models; ShareGPT, HumanEval | Higher goodput than compared systems under stated targets | Needs cluster resources |
| 23 | Pandey & Singh (2026a) | Measure continuity under provider failover | History-forwarding proxy; fault injection; LLM judge | Synthetic conversations | Continuity preserved far better than a stateless baseline | Preprint; synthetic; no streaming |
| 24 | Pandey & Singh (2026b) | Classify gateway failure modes | Layer × detectability taxonomy; reproductions | LiteLLM, Portkey, OpenRouter issue trackers | Worst failures are silent | Five entries; preprint; no prevalence data |
| 25 | Aderaldo & Mendonça (2026) | Evaluate retry and circuit-breaker patterns | Declarative fault scenarios on Kubernetes | Online Boutique; k6 | Maximum attempts drives success | Microservices, not LLM gateways |
| 26 | Miao et al. (2024) | Serve LLMs on preemptible instances | Dynamic reconfiguration and migration | Cloud spot instances | 2.4–9.1× lower P99 latency; 54% cost saving | Engine-level, not model fallback |
| 27 | Strati et al. (2024) | Fault-tolerant LLM serving | KV-cache streaming and replication | Multi-GPU serving | Faster recovery from failures | Needs engine state access |
| 28 | Hu et al. (2024) | Standard benchmark for routers | Stored responses; cost–quality plane | Over 405,000 inference outcomes; 11 LLMs | Oracle far above any single model | No latency or load; workshop paper |
| 29 | Li et al. (2026) | Unified current routing benchmark | Collector, evaluator, adaptor; ten routers | Over 400,000 instances; 21 datasets; 33 models | Many routers do not beat the best single model | Automated labels; public routers only |
| 30 | Zhuang et al. (2025) | Reusable compact model embeddings | Matrix factorisation on model×question correctness | 112 open models; 10 benchmarks | Outperforms nearest-neighbour baselines | Fixed pool; correctness only |
| 31 | Romero et al. (2021) | Model-less inference serving | Per-query variant selection; autoscaling | Image models on AWS | Lower cost and fewer SLO violations than compared systems | Image models only; no hosted APIs |
| 32 | Gujarati et al. (2020) | Predictable tail latency | Central scheduler from predicted durations | Image models; V100 cluster | Latency targets met for very high share of requests | Deterministic models; no fault tolerance |
| 33 | Griggs et al. (2024) | Minimise LLM serving cost across GPU types | Cost-aware bin packing over workload slices | Llama-2; L4, A10G, A100, H100 | Lower cost by workload type | Fixed workload; one model; preprint |
