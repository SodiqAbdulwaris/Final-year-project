### 2.4.7 Benchmarks and evaluation

Benchmark reuse will make a low-budget evaluation possible, but the meaning of a record, label and model pool will be checked first. The study will compare matched requests, separate validation from testing and report uncertainty. Automated labels will be treated as measurements with limitations rather than ground truth for every application.

**RouterBench** (Hu et al., 2024). *preprint.* Stored-outcome routing comparison will be considered in relation to Public scored multi-model tasks. RouterBench is a dataset of pre-computed LLM responses plus an evaluation framework, so that routers can be trained and compared without calling any model. It records each model's answer, correctness score and dollar cost for every prompt, and compares routers on a cost-quality plane with a single summary number, AIQ.

The relevant boundary is historical outputs/prices; preprint/workshop status. Already-scored outputs will reduce API spending, but contemporary latency and failures will require live tests. Outcome records will not be counted as unique requests.

**LLMRouterBench** (H. Li et al., 2026). *peer-reviewed.* Unified collection, scoring and router adaptation will be considered in relation to Multiple task and model pools. Its main finding is that many routers perform almost the same, several (including commercial ones) do not reliably beat a trivial baseline, and a large gap to the oracle remains.

The relevant boundary is pool/task alignment and latency-analysis assumptions. Its published version will support coverage checks and baseline comparisons. Model curation and a best-single reference will precede hybrid claims.

**Chatbot Arena** (Chiang et al., 2024). *peer-reviewed.* Pairwise human-preference comparison will be considered in relation to Crowdsourced model battles. Chatbot Arena is a public website where visitors ask any question to two anonymous LLMs and vote for the better answer. The paper describes the platform, shows how the votes are turned into model rankings with statistical confidence intervals, and checks that crowd votes are diverse and agree with experts.

The relevant boundary is preference population/task coverage. Preference rankings will not replace matched task scores. The project will use the quality definition appropriate to its selected dataset.

**RouterEval** (Z. Huang et al., 2025). *peer-reviewed.* Routing benchmark with varying model pools will be considered in relation to Model/task performance records. The authors also report a "model-level scaling up" effect: a good router's accuracy grows rapidly as more candidate models are added, even beating the best single model.

The relevant boundary is pool composition affects routing conclusions. The study will freeze its candidate pool and report its coverage instead of assuming results transfer to arbitrary added models.

**HELM** (Liang et al., 2023). *peer-reviewed.* Scenario-based multi-metric evaluation will be considered in relation to Language-model scenarios. All prompts and completions are released so that others can reuse them, and the toolkit is meant to be extended.

The relevant boundary is coverage/feasibility trade-offs. The report will separate quality, calibration and efficiency rather than compressing every outcome into a single favourable score.

**MT-Bench and LLM judging** (Zheng et al., 2023). *peer-reviewed.* The study compares automated judges with human preferences using chat tasks and preference votes.

The relevant boundary is position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases. Any judge-based score will document its rubric and limitations. A model's fluent answer will not be counted as reliable solely because a judge prefers it.

**EmbedLLM** (Zhuang et al., 2025). *peer-reviewed.* Learn compact model representations will be considered in relation to Model–question performance data. EmbedLLM learns one short vector for each LLM by training on a matrix of right/wrong results (which model answered which question correctly). The model vectors are combined with sentence-transformer question embeddings to predict correctness for new questions, to route queries and to predict benchmark accuracy.

The relevant boundary is representation learning depends on available outcomes. Query embeddings and model embeddings will be distinguished. The project will measure whether a simpler predictor meets the same practical need.

Taken together, these studies will guide an explicit baseline or design boundary. The proposal will test the selected mechanism under one frozen data and policy contract. It will not combine incompatible metrics or select a favourable operating point from each study as though they described one experiment.
