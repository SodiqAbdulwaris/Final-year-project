### 2.4.2 Prompt difficulty and suitability

Šakota et al. (2024) predict each candidate's success and assign requests under a budget, reporting 63% cost reduction in their setting. Xiong et al. (2024) instead examine confidence across five models and five dataset types, finding overconfidence; an answer-dependent confidence score is not automatically available before dispatch. Both make prediction quality consequential, but neither establishes gateway recovery.

Chuang et al. (2025) train a model to signal confidence after answering, while Zhu et al. (2025) use internal hidden states before generation. One changes the model and the other requires internal access. Damani et al. (2025) allocate inference computation by predicted benefit, whereas Zotos et al. (2025) estimate human exam-item difficulty. These targets are distinct from model-specific response quality; the proposed predictor must measure suitability rather than equate it with generic difficulty.
