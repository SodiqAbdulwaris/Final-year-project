### 2.4.3 Retrieval and k-NN routing

Historical retrieval will estimate suitability from related scored requests. The study will compare this component with a lightweight classifier and with their combination. Similarity will not guarantee shared solution requirements; coverage, leakage and calibration will remain evaluation concerns.

**Past-performance retrieval** (Varangot-Reille et al., 2026). *peer-reviewed.* Neighbour performance averaging and attention will be considered in relation to Routing benchmarks. The paper tests whether a router needs complex learned representations to handle new candidate models. Its ContextualRouter estimates each model's score on a new query from the scores of similar past queries, and a plain k-NN average over the same neighbours does as well or better.

The relevant boundary is historical label and retrieval coverage dependence. This is a close comparator for the embedding component. The project will explicitly test retrieval-only and no-retrieval variants instead of presuming a complex learner is superior.

**MTRouter** (Zhang et al., 2026). *peer-reviewed.* Joint history/model embeddings and outcome estimates will be considered in relation to ScienceWorld and HLE trajectories. MTRouter chooses which model to call at each turn of a long, tool-using agent episode, under a fixed cost budget. A small network predicts the final outcome of the episode for each candidate model given the interaction history so far, and the router picks the highest predicted outcome.

The relevant boundary is agent trajectories are not generic chat sessions. The work will inform the scope boundary. Session hysteresis and managed context will remain unresolved earlier proposals, rather than becoming mandatory experiments through citation.

Taken together, these studies will guide an explicit baseline or design boundary. The proposal will test the selected mechanism under one frozen data and policy contract. It will not combine incompatible metrics or select a favourable operating point from each study as though they described one experiment.
