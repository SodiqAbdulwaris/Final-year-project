### 2.4.1 Routing algorithms

Ding et al. (2024) reported up to 40% fewer large-model calls using a two-model quality-gap router, while Ong et al. (2025) trained on preferences and reported a 3.66-fold cost-saving ratio against GPT-4 at 95% of its MT-Bench quality. These use different baselines and outcomes. Both use a strong/weak decision, so neither establishes performance across an arbitrary pool or operational recovery.

RouterDC chooses among multiple candidates and reports 2.76% in-distribution and 1.90% out-of-distribution improvements (S. Chen et al., 2024); the weaker transfer and its 7B–8B model pool limit generalisation. Zooter uses reward supervision (Lu et al., 2024), whereas Shnitzer et al. (2023) use benchmark correctness: both depend on label coverage, and the latter's archival status remains unconfirmed.

FrugalGPT and AutoMix use an initial answer before escalation, unlike pre-generation selection (L. Chen et al., 2024; Aggarwal et al., 2024). FrugalGPT's preprint reports up to 98% cost reduction in a tested setting, but multiple calls add potential latency and final-version equivalence remains unchecked. LLM-Blender fuses multiple answers (Jiang et al., 2023), so its resource costs should not be confused with single-dispatch routing. The project should account for every generation and verification call.
