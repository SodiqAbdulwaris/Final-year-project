## 3.2 Requirements Elicitation

Requirements will be elicited from several sources, so that findings can be cross-checked.

- Document analysis. The project aim, the team’s recorded design decisions, the literature of Chapter Two and the documented behaviour of existing gateways (LiteLLM, n.d.; OpenRouter, n.d.; Portkey, n.d.) will show which functions such systems offer. Output: candidate requirements with a source for each.
- Inspection of benchmark data. Task and model coverage, quality labels and usage fields in RouterBench and LLMRouterBench will be examined (Hu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026). Output: data and reproducibility requirements.
- Scenario walkthroughs and prototyping. The team will walk through a normal request, an unaffordable request, a missing capability, provider throttling, a timeout, a malformed response and partial delivery, and will sketch the request interface and the operator view. Output: observable requirements and acceptance tests.

Participants are the six team members and the supervisor, and requirements are checked by review with the supervisor in sprint reviews. The instruments are a scenario checklist and the sketches. No questionnaire or interview is planned, because human participation will be minimal or absent, and no result from a population of users will be claimed. Findings will be recorded as requirements with identifiers and MoSCoW priorities (Section 3.5) and, for the main scenarios, as acceptance criteria in the form given, when and then, which become test cases in Chapter Four. If an optional participant exercise is added later, informed consent, anonymous responses and a documented instrument will come first, and the university’s ethics process will be followed (Section 3.10).
