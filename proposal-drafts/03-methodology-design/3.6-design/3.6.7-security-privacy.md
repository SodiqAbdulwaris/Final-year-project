### 3.6.7 Security and Privacy Design

The gateway will authenticate callers, separate administrator permissions and validate payload size, context and output caps and authorised endpoints. Deployment addresses are controlled by the administrator, and protections against server-side request forgery check schemes, destinations and redirects while allowing only explicitly authorised local endpoints. Provider credentials stay out of client responses, model prompts and routine logs. Input text is treated as untrusted data: instructions inside a request cannot change permissions, budget accounting, secret access or retry policy. Calls with side effects need an explicit safe-retry contract, because a transport failure does not show that an earlier action did not happen.

The project will keep the data it retains to a minimum, use anonymous request identifiers and prefer aggregate records. Raw personal or confidential data will not be collected on purpose or sent to hosted models, and logs and exports will be redacted. The Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023 is considered in the design. Its principles of personal data processing (section 24) include lawful processing, purpose limitation, data minimisation and storage limitation, and sections 41 to 43 govern transfers of personal data out of Nigeria (Federal Republic of Nigeria, 2023). These are regulatory requirements and not a claim that compliance has been achieved. If personal data is ever processed, the team will document its lawful basis, notices, access and deletion process, recipient restrictions and any cross-border arrangement before collection, and will not infer these from a provider merely offering an API.

Security tests will include unauthenticated calls, privilege violations, forbidden endpoints, oversized inputs, secret and redaction checks, and fallback attempts that would break privacy or budget policy. The absence of formal ethics approval does not replace privacy or security obligations.
