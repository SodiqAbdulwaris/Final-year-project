## 1.8 Limitations

The model pool will be small and restricted by benchmark coverage. Already-scored outputs may refer to historical versions, prompts and prices that differ from current hosted services. A model listed in a benchmark will not automatically have aligned outcomes with every other candidate. Reuse will therefore require an intersection of comparable task records rather than substitution of aggregate scores.

Compute will be limited to two RTX 4070 laptops (a Lenovo Legion 7i and an ASUS ROG Zephyrus G16). Laptop thermal and power limits may reduce sustained performance. Their memory capacity, host memory, operating systems and sustained performance will remain [PLACEHOLDER: measured hardware specifications]. Quantisation or context limits may be needed for small local models, and such variants will not be assumed equivalent to benchmark deployments. The minimum API budget and a small hosted-call allowance will restrict pilot size and the precision of live timing estimates.

Stored outputs will support quality/cost replay but will not recreate present-day latency, throttling or provider failures. Offline findings will consequently be separated from live measurements. Small failure-injection tests will demonstrate behaviour in the stated scenarios, not a general availability guarantee.

Scoring labels may reflect benchmark conventions, automated judges or incomplete task coverage. Similarity features may be less useful on unfamiliar requests; calibration will be assessed rather than presumed. Deduplication and grouped splits will reduce leakage, but will not prove that the underlying models have never encountered benchmark material.

The literature review will be structured rather than exhaustive. Some sources will remain preprints or abstract-level readings, and unsupported detailed claims will be withheld. Vendor documents will provide dated feature evidence, not independent performance validation. The team will need to read key papers directly and record how AI-assisted summaries were checked.

Human-participant work will be minimal or absent. This will constrain claims about usability and user acceptance. No inference about a broad developer population will be drawn from informal team review.
