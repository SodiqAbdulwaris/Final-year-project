## 1.2 Statement of the Problem

**What is.** The original project proposal identifies fixed-model integration as the motivating application scenario. A fixed choice will provide a useful baseline, but will not adapt its model choice to the request. Research already addresses learned routing and cascading (Ding et al., 2024; Ong et al., 2025; L. Chen et al., 2024). The existence of those methods means that implementing model choice alone will not establish a research contribution. The project will need to establish whether its chosen combination justifies its added complexity.

**What ought to be.** A request should reach a model that can process it, is available and is affordable under the declared policy. Among feasible candidates, selection should account for estimated quality and latency. Hard capability, budget and privacy restrictions should continue to apply during recovery. The client should receive an explicit infeasibility or execution error when no permitted action remains, rather than an apparently successful response that silently violates a restriction.

**The gap and its consequences.** This project investigates whether combining lightweight classification and embedding-derived suitability features with constrained cost–quality–latency selection improves on simpler routing baselines. Operational fallback is evaluated separately for resilience. Without this controlled comparison, additional predictor components might increase overhead without useful savings, and successful offline routing might conceal unreliable execution. The contribution will be the formal decision policy, integration and empirical evaluation. No first-of-its-kind or fundamentally-new-algorithm claim will be made.

This project therefore seeks to design and evaluate a hybrid prompt-aware request router that chooses eligible LLM deployments under quality, cost and latency constraints, and to assess its operational fallback separately under injected execution failures.
