## 1.2 Statement of the Problem

**What is.** Many applications send every request to one fixed model. A fixed choice is a useful baseline, but it cannot adapt to the request, even though deployments differ in cost, latency, capability and availability (Section 1.1). Learned routing and cascading are already studied (L. Chen et al., 2024; Ding et al., 2024; Ong et al., 2025), so implementing model selection alone would not be a research contribution. Most comparisons of routing methods are also made offline on stored outputs (Hu et al., 2024), which cannot show whether a working system enforces its constraints and recovers from failures. Nigerian teams meet two further pressures: dollar-priced services and rules on moving personal data abroad.

**What ought to be.** A request should reach a model that can process it, is available and is affordable under the declared policy. Among the feasible candidates, selection should account for estimated quality and latency. Hard capability, budget and privacy restrictions should still apply during recovery. When no permitted action remains, the client should receive an explicit infeasibility or execution error, not an apparently successful response that silently breaks a restriction.

**The gap and its consequences.** Without a controlled comparison, additional predictor components may add overhead without useful savings, and successful offline routing may hide unreliable execution. The people affected are developers and organisations that integrate several LLM deployments, including Nigerian teams with limited budgets. This project investigates whether combining lightweight classification and embedding-derived suitability features with constrained cost–quality–latency selection improves on simpler routing baselines. Operational fallback is evaluated separately for resilience. The contribution will be the decision policy, its integration into a working gateway and an empirical evaluation. No claim of being the first or a fundamentally new algorithm will be made.

This project therefore seeks to design, implement and evaluate a hybrid prompt-aware request-routing gateway that chooses eligible LLM deployments under quality, cost and latency constraints, and to assess its operational fallback separately under injected execution failures.
