## 1.5 Methodology Overview

The project will follow an iterative artefact-development and controlled-evaluation approach. Requirements will be extracted from the project design, the application scenarios and the reviewed literature. A minimal gateway with validation, a deployment registry and model adapters will be built first. Fixed-model, cheapest-eligible and rule-based policies will give the initial comparisons before the learned components are added. The integrated gateway will then be deployed in a documented environment, and functional, integration and failure-handling tests will check it against its requirements.

Already-scored outputs from RouterBench and LLMRouterBench will be considered for offline predictor development and replay evaluation (Hu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026). Their versions, model coverage, task overlap and licence conditions will be checked before use. Requests will be split into training, validation and test sets, and evaluated outcomes will be kept out of the retrieval index used to route those requests. The hybrid predictor will combine task classification with embedding-derived features, and ablations will isolate the value of each. Deterministic eligibility filtering will come before selection, and the selector will prefer the cheapest feasible model. A capped live pilot and controlled failure injection on local models will measure latency, gateway overhead and fallback behaviour, which replayed outputs cannot show. Results will report quality, cost, latency, calibration, constraint violations and fallback completion with uncertainty intervals where the sample allows, and all tuning will precede the held-out analysis.
